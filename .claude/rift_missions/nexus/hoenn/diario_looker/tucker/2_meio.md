> The day the lights were paid for.
>
> The Dome was sold. The new owner wore a very clean suit and spoke about the next thousand years. He paid in electricity.
>
> The power comes from under the pitch. Something long and pale, asleep, that drinks whatever is poured into the stadium. Light. Noise. Cheering.
>
> Backstage, Tucker showed me his props. A rusted sword and a rusted shield. He polishes them before every show.
>
> They were meant for two children. The children never came. Neither did a young man in a cape, who was supposed to stop the dark day.
>
> In the margin, another hand: “Not props. Keys. The vibration changes when he holds them.”
>
> Tucker put them back in their case. A star never lets the audience see the stagehands, he said.

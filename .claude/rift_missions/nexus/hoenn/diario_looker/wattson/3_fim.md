> September 31st. The clocks are running.
>
> Wattson fixed the pocket watch this morning. One spare gear, from the pink one. It offered. When the watch began to tick, every clock in Mauville ticked with it.
>
> The minute ended. Nothing exploded. The generator had been safe for years. The two of them had seen to it.
>
> He had been afraid to check -- no. He knew. He kept the minute because it was the one in which the kid from the workshop said goodbye. Heavy vibration. He liked that minute.
>
> He laughed for a long time. Then he cried a little. Then he laughed again.
>
> The pocket watch is gone. A man in a long coat asked him the time on the way out. Wattson lent him the watch. He did not get it back.
>
> The man wore a coat like mine. Longer. I have decided that is a coincidence.
